Network Audio Grabber & Sender

Listen to network audio streams on a Mac, and send a Mac's audio to other machines. Free software under the MIT licence. This page takes five minutes and saves most first-day questions.

What's in the disk image

ItemWhat it is
Network Audio GrabberThe receiver. Finds and plays AES67, RAVENNA, Dante (in AES67 mode) and VBAN streams, mixes several at once, with meters, loudness and Audio Unit inserts. It can also send this Mac's audio (its Send area).
Network Audio SenderA small app that sends whatever this Mac is playing, as VBAN, to Grabbers or any VBAN receiver (Voicemeeter, for example).
DAT Audio Plug-ins (beta)Optional. Three Audio Unit plug-ins for DAWs: Grabber, Grabber (effect) and Sender.

Each app has an illustrated guide under Help → Quick Start Guide, and its credits and licences under About.

Before you start

  • A Mac with Apple silicon (M1 or later) running macOS 14.2 Sonoma or later. Intel Macs are not supported by this build.
  • It isn't from the App Store and isn't notarised by Apple, so macOS asks you to confirm the first time you open it (step 2).
  • Nothing is sent to the internet or across the network unless you choose to send something. There are no accounts, analytics or update checks.

1Install the apps

  1. Drag Network Audio Grabber and, if you want it, Network Audio Sender onto Applications in the disk image's window.
  2. Eject the disk image, and open the apps from your Applications folder.

2Opening them the first time

If macOS opens the app straight away, skip this step. Otherwise:

macOS 15 Sequoia and later

  1. Double-click the app. macOS says it could not verify the app. Click Done (not Move to Bin).
  2. Open System Settings → Privacy & Security and scroll down to Security. Next to the message about the app, click Open Anyway.
  3. Enter your password, then click Open Anyway again. You only do this once per app.

macOS 14 Sonoma

  1. Control-click (or right-click) the app in Applications and choose Open.
  2. Click Open in the dialog. You only do this once per app.
Comfortable with Terminal? This does the same for both apps at once: 
xattr -dr com.apple.quarantine "/Applications/Network Audio Grabber.app" "/Applications/Network Audio Sender.app"

3Permissions macOS will ask for

WhenThe questionAnswer
First launch of either appAllow … to find devices on local networks?Allow. Without it the Grabber cannot find RAVENNA or Dante streams, and the apps can't find each other.
If the macOS firewall is onAccept incoming network connections?Allow. Streams arrive as incoming network traffic.
The first time something is sent (the Sender, or the Grabber's Send)Permission to record this Mac's audioAllow. Until then nothing is captured; the window says so and has a button to the right page of System Settings (Privacy & Security → Screen & System Audio Recording → System Audio Recording Only).

You can change any of these later in System Settings → Privacy & Security.

4The DAW plug-ins (optional, beta)

The simple way

  1. Quit your DAW.
  2. Open the Grabber and choose Help → Install DAW Plug-ins (beta)…. They go in your own Components folder; no password, no Terminal. The same menu item updates or removes them later.
  3. Open the DAW and let it rescan. In Logic, if they don't appear: Logic Pro → Settings → Plug-in Manager → Reset & Rescan Selection.

Or from the disk image, for every user of this Mac

  1. Drag DAT Audio Plug-ins onto Components in the disk image's window, and enter your password when asked.
  2. A DAW can't show the "open anyway" question for a plug-in, so clear the download flag in Terminal: 
    sudo xattr -dr com.apple.quarantine "/Library/Audio/Plug-Ins/Components/DAT Audio Plug-ins.component"
PluginUse it as
DAT Audio: GrabberAn instrument: plays network streams on its track. Logic: a Software Instrument track. REAPER: instrument FX.
DAT Audio: Grabber (effect)An effect that puts the network in place of the track, for hosts that load only effects (Audacity, Audition, Premiere Pro, Final Cut Pro, Resolve, Audio Hijack). In Logic, use it on an audio track with input monitoring on to hear the network continuously.
DAT Audio: SenderAn effect that sends its track over VBAN to a Grabber or any VBAN receiver.

So far the plug-ins have been tried mainly in REAPER. Cubase, Nuendo, Pro Tools and Reason don't load Audio Units.

5Getting going

  • Open Help → Quick Start Guide in either app. It covers listening, the four ways to send, and the window.
  • Listening to AES67 / RAVENNA / Dante: pick the network interface that's on the audio network (top left of the Grabber), then press ▶ on a stream. Dante devices need AES67 mode switched on in Dante Controller.
  • Sending between two Macs on the same network: in the Sender, turn on Findable by Grabbers. The stream appears in the other Mac's Grabber; press ▶ there, then Allow on the Sender.
  • Between two places (two buildings, across the internet): use Connect / Send… → Somewhere else → Remote link. Each end makes a code and sends it to the other. Nothing to set up on a router, and the audio is encrypted end to end.

Good to know

  • Where the sound goes. The Grabber plays through the Mac's current output device and follows it, unless you choose one: click the device name at the top of the Grabber (or Settings → Output → Device). A chosen device stays chosen; if it's unplugged, the Grabber plays on the current one until it's back.
  • Channels. A stream can have up to 64 channels. The Grabber plays in stereo to begin with: each playing stream's Bus menu sets how it gets there — a fold-down of the whole stream (5.1 → 2.0, say), one pair of its channels, or one channel in mono. For surround, Settings → Output → Output bus can be 4.0, 5.1, 7.1 or the device's full width. Settings → Fold-down sets how the fold-down mixes.
  • What gets sent. System audio to begin with: everything this Mac plays, except the two apps themselves. The Source menu (the Sender's header, or under the stream name in the Grabber's Send) changes that to chosen apps — a browser's sound comes from its helper processes, which are included with it — or an input device (a microphone, an interface's inputs, BlackHole), sent whole at its own channel count. macOS asks for Microphone access the first time.
  • Routing ideas. Several devices at once: a Multi-Output Device from Audio MIDI Setup, chosen as the Grabber's output. Into another app: choose BlackHole (free) as the Grabber's output and as that app's input. A mix of several things to send: route them into BlackHole or an aggregate device, then choose it as the Sender's input. Rogue Amoeba's Loopback (paid) does the same with a visual router.

Good network manners

  • Nothing sends at launch unless you have chosen to (and ticked Resume sending when opened).
  • Broadcast sends to every device on a network. It's fine on a small network of your own, but please don't use it on a busy shared network. Findable by Grabbers or a single address is the polite choice there.
  • If you are on a managed network (a company or venue), follow its rules on streaming audio. Normal monitoring of AES67 streams adds no traffic beyond what you choose to listen to.
  • Audio on the local network (VBAN, AES67) is not encrypted, like other tools of this kind. Only the Remote link is encrypted. By address across the internet is not encrypted and needs a router port opened; the apps warn you before you use it.

Early and beta parts

  • Network Audio Sender and VBAN receive are newer than the AES67 side, and have been tested on fewer Macs.
  • Beta, off by default (Settings → Discovery): Scream (a Windows virtual sound card), RTP without PTP (PipeWire, PulseAudio, VLC, GStreamer) and Internet streams (Icecast/SHOUTcast radio). Try them if you're curious, but they're not the focus.
  • There is no Windows version yet.

Things that look like faults but aren't

You seeWhy
AES67 streams are listed but play nothingThe wrong network interface is chosen. Multicast arrives only on the interface that's on the audio network. The log says so after a few seconds.
Nothing from the audio network over Wi-Fi or a VPNWi-Fi is usually a different subnet, and remote-access VPNs don't carry multicast. VBAN and the Remote link do work over a VPN or the internet.
A Dante Virtual Soundcard stream never appearsDante Virtual Soundcard and Dante Via can't send AES67 at all.
In Logic, the Grabber instrument goes quiet when its track isn't selectedLogic only renders a Software Instrument track while it's selected or armed. Use the Grabber (effect) on an audio track with input monitoring for continuous listening.
The app and a Grabber plugin are both open, and one of them misses a streamThey share VBAN's port. Streams sent straight to this Mac reach whichever started first; Findable Senders reach both.
"underruns" climbing, little clicksThe buffer is too small for that network. Raise it in Settings → Playout (AES67 starts at 15 ms; Wi-Fi and the internet need more). Test the connection suggests a figure for remote streams.

If something goes wrong

Each app keeps a log of what it did and why: Settings → Log, with Export… to save it as a text file. It includes network addresses and stream names from your network, so share it only with someone you trust to help.

Updating to a newer build

Quit the apps (and any DAW using the plug-ins), then drag the new apps onto Applications and replace them. Your settings and permissions carry over. Then Help → Update DAW Plug-ins… in the Grabber, if you use the plug-ins.

Removing everything

  1. In either app: Settings → Remove all settings… (in the Grabber it's under Settings → Log). It clears both apps' settings and the plug-ins', then quits. Quit the other app and any DAW using the plug-ins first.
  2. If you installed the plug-ins: Help → Remove DAW Plug-ins… in the Grabber (or, if installed for all users, remove DAT Audio Plug-ins from /Library/Audio/Plug-Ins/Components).
  3. Move both apps to the Bin.
  4. Empty the Bin. macOS drops the apps' permissions by itself.
